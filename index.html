      Marble Ludo

      Four marbles each, one die you throw yourself. Throw it too hard and it leaves the board.

        Rules at this table

        Throwing the die

        On your turn the die waits over your yard. Press it, carry it, and flick to throw. It has to roll: a die you only drop does not count. If it leaves the board, that throw is a loss and your throwing ends, whatever it shows. Sixes you threw before the loss are still counted. A table can switch losses off; the die then bounces off the rim and always counts.

        Moving

        A 6 earns another throw, and nothing is counted yet. You may keep throwing until something other than a 6 comes up, or stop after any 6 and count what you hold. Then count every die you threw, one at a time, in any order and on any marbles. Within one turn a marble keeps to one direction: once it has gone forward it cannot come backward to eliminate, and the other way round. A marble that has not moved this turn can. A marble that eliminates is finished for the turn. Every die you threw has to be counted: if no order lets you count them all, you have no move that turn. So with a 6 and a 2, a marble may use the 6 to eliminate only if another marble can take the 2, and a marble five squares from the centre cannot retire on a 5 if you also hold a 6 that nothing can count. Stopping after a 6 avoids picking up a die you cannot use. A 6 can bring a marble out of your yard onto your start square. Press a marble to see every square it can reach, then press the square you want; nothing moves until you choose. At the mouth of your home lane you decide whether to turn in or carry on round the track. Reach the centre by exact count. If you have a legal move you must take one.

        Eliminating

        Land exactly on a single opposing marble to send it back to its yard. Three kinds of move exist only for eliminating:

        Backward. From anywhere on the board, home lanes included, a marble may count backward if it lands exactly on an opponent. From a lane it counts back out to the track and on.

        Parallel. When the square you count onto, forward or backward, is straight across an arm from a lone opponent, with exactly the home-lane square between, you may eliminate that marble. Your marble takes its square and carries on from there, which can put it much nearer home. Coming out on a 6 counts: an opponent across the home lane from your start square can be taken. A marble inside a home lane can never be taken this way.

        Into a home. A marble may turn into an opponent's home lane, or step from their start square into their yard, if the count lands exactly on a marble there. Yard dimples count 1 to 4 from the start square. A marble hit in its own yard leaves the game. The raider walks back out on later moves.

        Stacks

        Two or more marbles of one colour on a square are a stack. A stack cannot be eliminated, and no marble of any colour can pass over it.

        Laborer

        If your last marble on the board is eliminated, you become a laborer for the player who did it. You keep throwing on your turn, but every number that is not a 6 is played by them on their marbles. Throw a 6 and you bring a marble out and are free; any other dice from that turn are then yours.

        Winning

        Bring every marble you still have to the centre. A player who loses all four marbles in yard raids is out.
